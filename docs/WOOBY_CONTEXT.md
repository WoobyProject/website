## What Wooby is

Wooby is an open-source, technology-driven initiative created to tackle infant and child malnutrition through accessible, high-precision weight monitoring tools.

At its core, Wooby is a low-cost, high-precision infant scale designed to help healthcare workers, NGOs, and communities monitor children's growth and support earlier detection of malnutrition risks.

Wooby is not only a device. It is also a collaborative open-source innovation model that brings together humanitarian organizations, healthcare experts, volunteers, engineers, designers, prototyping labs, and potential local makers to create practical technology for child health.

## Core mission

Wooby's mission is to harness technology, innovation, and collaboration to make high-precision children's weight monitoring accessible anywhere in the world.

As an open-source initiative, Wooby aims to empower communities, healthcare workers, and NGOs with tools to detect and prevent malnutrition earlier, because every child deserves to grow strong and healthy.

## Vision

Wooby envisions a world where communities everywhere can access the tools they need to monitor children's growth, identify malnutrition risks early, and act before preventable health problems become severe.

## The problem

Child malnutrition remains a major global health challenge, especially for children under five in low-resource settings.

Nutritional monitoring is often limited by:

- infrequent measurements
- lack of high-precision equipment
- limited access to clinical-grade tools
- insufficient data granularity
- poor data management tools
- operational constraints in field settings

High-precision infant scales are often expensive, clinically oriented, or difficult to calibrate and maintain. Low-cost models may fail to meet the precision needed for certain use cases. This creates a gap between affordability and reliable measurement.

Wooby was conceived to address that gap.

## The solution

Wooby creates accessible, high-precision infant weight monitoring technology.

The current product is a low-cost baby scale with:

- 20 kg capacity
- 5 gram precision up to 10 kg
- estimated production cost of approximately USD 50 to 70 in the current volunteer-led configuration
- 3D-printed components
- off-the-shelf electronic parts
- globally available components
- software compensation for limitations of budget sensors
- calibration routines
- signal processing
- machine learning possibilities

The project also plans to develop software capabilities to collect, analyze, transmit, and act on weight data for better health insights.

## Product maturity

Wooby should be presented as a field-tested and evolving open-source innovation, not as a finished mass-market medical device.

Current status:

- born in 2020 within the Airbus Humanity Lab
- developed by a multidisciplinary volunteer team
- supported by rapid prototyping infrastructure
- tested in pilot deployments in South Sudan and Colombia
- preparing further refinement, open-source publication, and connected data features

Avoid suggesting that Wooby is already fully commercialized, clinically certified, or universally deployed unless the project documentation explicitly supports that claim.

## Origin and collaboration model

Wooby was born within the Airbus Humanity Lab, a philanthropic-minded volunteer network where mainly Airbus employees contribute expertise to positive-impact innovation projects.

The project emerged from a call inviting NGOs to present challenges where technology innovation could make a meaningful difference.

Key ecosystem actors include:

- MEDAIR, which proposed the urgent need for child malnutrition monitoring tools and supported field testing in South Sudan
- Sociedad Colombiana de Pediatria, which joined the project in 2024 and supported testing in Colombia
- Airbus ProtoSpace, which provided prototyping infrastructure
- volunteer engineers and designers, including electronics, software, 3D design, UX, product, and stakeholder management profiles
- potential future nodes such as universities, FabLabs, maker spaces, and local social entrepreneurs

This collaboration model is central to the identity of Wooby.

## Pilot deployments and validation

Initial pilot deployments took place in 2024 and 2025.

Known field deployments include:

- two units delivered to Juba and Pibor, South Sudan, with MEDAIR
- one unit delivered to Barranquilla, Colombia, through a partnership with the Sociedad Colombiana de Pediatria

Across both deployment regions, Wooby has been used with 60 infants aged between 9 days and 12 months.

Reported testing context:

- 42 infants in South Sudan identified as severely malnourished, below the minus 3 sigma curve for weight-for-age
- 18 infants in Colombia, tested with healthy, non-malnourished children in clinical settings
- longitudinal weight data collected by the MEDAIR team over a month
- positive feedback from health workers on ease of use and integration into routine protocols

Use these details carefully. They are valuable credibility signals, but should not be turned into unsupported claims of medical certification or guaranteed clinical outcomes.

## Open-source strategy

Wooby is designed to be:

- open-source
- replicable
- adaptable
- affordable
- locally buildable
- suitable for low-resource settings
- compatible with decentralized fabrication

The open-source approach is intended to let partners worldwide build, adapt, improve, and maintain the device using accessible materials and standard equipment.

The project can enable multiple forms of distributed entrepreneurship and local value creation, including:

- local device production
- servicing
- adaptation
- training
- applied research
- integration into NGO health workflows

## Scalability strategy

Wooby's scalability is based on a distributed ecosystem rather than a conventional proprietary startup model.

Scaling may happen through:

- NGOs integrating Wooby into care pathways
- local makers building or adapting the device
- universities using Wooby for training and applied research
- prototyping labs supporting fabrication and improvement
- social entrepreneurs creating services around production, maintenance, or deployment
- open-source contributors improving the design and software

This model democratizes access to technological solutions while creating a collaborative impact ecosystem.

## Product framing

It is acceptable to describe Wooby as:

- an open-source baby scale
- a low-cost, high-precision infant scale
- an affordable child weight monitoring tool
- a high-precision weight monitoring solution
- a technology-driven tool for earlier malnutrition detection and monitoring
- a collaborative open-source innovation for child health

Be careful with claims such as:

- medically certified
- clinically validated
- guaranteed to prevent malnutrition
- diagnostic device
- eradicates malnutrition
- proven at scale

Only use those claims if there is evidence and approval to support them.
